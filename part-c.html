PART C

Source Evaluation

The Rare and Excellent History of Saladin — Bahāʾ al-Dīn Ibn Shaddād

AT A GLANCE

TYPE
Primary source
AUTHOR
Bahāʾ al-Dīn Ibn Shaddād
JOINED SALADIN
1188
RELATIONSHIP
Knew Saladin personally, close to his court

The source I have chosen to evaluate is The Rare and Excellent History of Saladin by Bahāʾ al-Dīn Ibn Shaddād. It is a primary source because Ibn Shaddād lived during Saladin’s lifetime and knew him personally. He joined Saladin’s service in 1188 and stayed close to him and his court. This gave him the chance to see Saladin and the events around him directly, rather than learning about them hundreds of years later.

The main purpose of the source was to record the life, actions and character of Saladin. It gives information about his leadership, military campaigns and personal qualities. Because Ibn Shaddād was close to Saladin, the source can give historians details that may not be found in more distant accounts. It is especially useful for understanding how Saladin behaved and how people around him viewed him.

However, the source is not completely reliable on its own. Ibn Shaddād admired Saladin, so his writing can give a more positive view of him. This creates bias because he may have focused more on Saladin’s good qualities and achievements than on his mistakes or weaknesses. His position close to Saladin could also have affected what he was able to say. This does not mean that the source is false, but historians need to be careful about treating everything in it as completely neutral.

The source is still very valuable because it was created close to the events it describes and by someone who personally knew Saladin. It can help historians understand Saladin’s leadership, personality and reputation during his own time. It can also provide information about the political and religious world he lived in.

Overall, I think Ibn Shaddād’s source is very useful for studying Saladin, but it should not be used by itself. His close relationship with Saladin makes the source valuable, while his admiration for him is an important limitation. Comparing it with other primary and secondary sources allows historians to check the information and build a more balanced understanding of Saladin.

Weighing it up
